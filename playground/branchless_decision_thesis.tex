\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Ultra-high-frequency trading is decision-making under a hard microsecond deadline. In
that regime the quality of a decision is bounded not by how well an engine can think but
by whether it can act before the opportunity closes. This thesis presents a class of
\emph{constant-time, data-oblivious decision engines} engineered for that regime, and
proves that they occupy a part of the latency--quality space that adaptive search engines
cannot reach. The engines have three defining properties: their decision latency is
invariant to input complexity, so it does not degrade during volatility; their worst-case
execution time is exact and certifiable; and every decision emits a constant-time
process-conformance receipt, making execution auditable. We validate the engines in a
fully adversarial, zero-sum setting---chess against a calibrated, world-class
opponent---and report a $104$-game latency sweep showing the predicted strength curve
$\Elo(\budget)$, including parity with minimal-time Stockfish at $10\,\mathrm{ms}$ and
coherent, sometimes winning, play at $3\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ on a quarter of the opponent's
total compute. Below the opponent's $58\,\mu\mathrm{s}$ search floor the engine is the
sole feasible player. We then derive the underlying mathematics from first principles: a
\emph{branchless algebra} that eliminates data-dependent control flow, a \emph{calculus of
data-oblivious latency} that yields zero-variance timing and a tight worst-case bound, and
the \emph{geometry of the latency--quality manifold}, whose central result---the Inversion
Theorem---establishes that a constant-time engine uniquely occupies the sub-feasibility
band of any search engine and reaches parity at a definite budget. We introduce
\emph{Decision-Engine Elo}, a latency-parameterized skill rating that the trading industry
presently lacks, on which constant-time engines lead in the microsecond band by
construction.

\chapter{The Latency Regime}\label{ch:regime}

\section{The deadline is the design constraint}
A trade is a decision with an expiry. A quote lives for microseconds; an imbalance signal
decays before a millisecond elapses; a marketable order that arrives late is filled at a
worse price or not at all. The binding constraint of ultra-high-frequency trading (UHFT)
is therefore not analytical depth but \emph{latency}: the decision must be emitted inside
the opportunity's lifetime. An engine that produces a superior decision after the deadline
has produced nothing of value.

\section{Depth is the wrong objective below the deadline}
The dominant paradigm of automated decision-making spends time to buy quality: search
depth grows logarithmically in allotted time and strength grows linearly in depth
(Proposition~\ref{prop:searchlaw}). This is optimal when time is abundant and a liability
when it is not. Two structural facts make depth-seeking unsuitable for UHFT.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=2em]
  \item \textbf{A hard feasibility floor.} Every search engine has a least budget
        $\budget_{\min}$ below which it cannot complete even a one-step lookahead and emits
        no action (Proposition~\ref{prop:searchlaw}). Below $\budget_{\min}$ the
        depth-seeking architecture is not slow---it is \emph{absent}.
  \item \textbf{Latency that worsens with volatility.} A data-dependent engine's latency
        rises with the entropy of its inputs (Theorem~\ref{thm:entropy},
        Proposition~\ref{prop:vol}). Its slowest decisions occur during the most chaotic
        markets---the news spike, the liquidity withdrawal---which are precisely the
        moments when speed is most valuable.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The constant-time alternative}
A \emph{data-oblivious} engine executes the same operations regardless of its input. Its
latency is constant in input complexity (Proposition~\ref{prop:vol}), its worst-case
execution time is exact (Proposition~\ref{prop:zerovar}), and its decision cost collapses
to a single evaluation, placing it far below any search engine's feasibility floor
(Proposition~\ref{prop:plateau}). The remainder of Part~I states the three capabilities
this delivers; Parts~II and~III validate and derive them.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\chapter{Capabilities of the Constant-Time Engine}\label{ch:capabilities}

\section{Volatility-invariant latency}
The engine's per-decision latency does not depend on the state of the market. Formally its
realized latency surface $\Lambda(\budget,\kvol)$ is flat in the complexity coordinate
$\kvol$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:vol}); branchy competitors have a surface that tilts upward
in $\kvol$. The tail-latency advantage of the engine is the integral of that tilt over the
volatility distribution. Operationally: the engine's $p99$ latency tracks its median, and
neither moves when the order book turns chaotic.

\section{Provable worst-case execution time}
Because the engine is data-oblivious, its worst-case execution time equals its constant
per-decision cost and is attained on every input (Proposition~\ref{prop:zerovar}). This
yields a certifiable guarantee of the form ``every decision completes within $c(n)$
cycles, for any input,'' a hard real-time bound. Data-dependent systems admit no tight
such bound, because their worst case is a pathological misprediction sequence that
standard testing does not exhibit.

\section{Auditable execution}
Every decision traverses a fixed pipeline---\textsf{Perceive}, \textsf{Evaluate},
\textsf{Search}, \textsf{Select}, \textsf{Commit}---encoded as a Petri net
$\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{dec}}$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:conformance}). The engine emits a
hash-chained event per stage; replaying the log through $\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{dec}}$
returns conformance fitness $\fit=1$ for a lawful execution and $\fit<1$ for any skipped
or reordered stage. The replay is itself data-oblivious, so the \emph{audit} carries the
same tight worst-case bound as the decision. Each decision therefore ships with a
constant-time, cryptographically chained proof that it followed the lawful process.

\section{The microsecond band is single-occupancy}
The occupancy function $\occ(\budget)$ collects the engines feasible within budget
$\budget$; it is monotone and its low-latency limit retains only data-oblivious engines
(Proposition~\ref{prop:occ}, Corollary~\ref{cor:empty}). In the band below a search
engine's feasibility floor, the constant-time engine is the only player able to act.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\chapter{Decision-Engine Elo}\label{ch:eloTau}

\section{A rating the industry lacks}
Trading engines are evaluated by profit-and-loss, Sharpe ratio, and drawdown: absolute,
regime-dependent, capital-confounded quantities that are not comparable across firms or
epochs. Chess has a standardized, head-to-head, relative skill rating---Elo. Its trading
analogue does not exist. We define it.

\begin{definition}[Decision-Engine Elo]\label{def:eloTau}
For two engines in a shared zero-sum environment at a common latency budget $\budget$, let
$\score_{\Engine\Engine'}(\budget)$ be the expected score of $\Engine$ against $\Engine'$.
Decision-Engine Elo is the latency-parameterized rating $\Elo_\Engine(\budget)$ obeying
\[
  \score_{\Engine\Engine'}(\budget)=
  \frac{1}{1+10^{-(\Elo_\Engine(\budget)-\Elo_{\Engine'}(\budget))/400}},
\]
fitted from head-to-head play at each $\budget$ and anchored once.
\end{definition}

\begin{axiom}[Latency primacy]\label{ax:primacy}
An engine's rating is the curve $\budget\mapsto\Elo_\Engine(\budget)$, not a scalar.
\end{axiom}

\begin{proposition}[Ranking inversion]\label{prop:rankinv}
If two engines' curves cross at $\budget_c$, their rank order reverses across $\budget_c$.
A scalar rating is therefore order-violating; only the curve is well-defined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Across $\budget_c$ the sign of $\Elo_\Engine-\Elo_{\Engine'}$ flips, and the logistic law
assigns the majority of expected score to the higher-rated engine on each side.
\end{proof}

By Corollary~\ref{cor:empty} and Proposition~\ref{prop:occ}, the low-$\budget$ region of
the Decision-Engine Elo chart admits a single architecture. The benchmark that measures
skill-per-microsecond is the benchmark on which the constant-time engine leads.

\part{Chess as Instrument}
% ######################################################################

\chapter{The Apparatus}\label{ch:engine}

Chess supplies a clean, adversarial, zero-sum, reproducible signal against a calibrated
and world-class opponent. It is the micrometer with which we measure the quantities Part~I
asserts and Part~III derives.

\section{The evaluator as an affine form}
A state $x$ projects to a sparse feature word $\psi(x)\in\Word{m}$. With first layer
$W\in\Reals^{h\times m}$ and bias $c\in\Reals^h$, the accumulator $a(x)=W\psi(x)+c$ is the
sum of the columns of $W$ indexed by the set bits of $\psi(x)$, computed branchlessly and
padded to maximal popcount so that its worst-case cost is exact.

\begin{lemma}[Split-ReLU signed identity]\label{lem:crelu}
For all $e\in\Reals$, $\relu(e)-\relu(-e)=e$, with $\relu(t)=\max(t,0)$ realized
branchlessly via the sign bit $t\wedge\neg(t\gg(w{-}1))$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$e\ge0$: $\relu(e)-\relu(-e)=e-0=e$. $e<0$: $0-(-e)=e$.
\end{proof}

Pairing a neuron with its mirror ($a_1=-a_0$, value weights $v=(+1,-1,0,\dots)$) lets a
rectifier value head return the signed evaluation $a_0$ with no branch. This is how the
engine's evaluation head computes a signed material--positional score.

\section{Divergence-free batched evaluation}
Data-obliviousness means $N$ states evaluate in lockstep with no lane divergence; on a
$40$-core single-instruction-multiple-data device this gives throughput $\Theta(N)$ per
dispatch. The kernel sustains $1.77\times10^{7}$ evaluations per second standalone.
Leaf-parallel batching with virtual loss restores $\sim 1.5\times10^{6}$ evaluations per
second \emph{inside} a tree search that would otherwise be dispatch-latency bound.

\section{Two search operators}
On the branchless evaluator we mount (a) a negamax alpha--beta search with quiescence, a
transposition table, and reductions, and (b) a Monte-Carlo tree search with PUCT selection
and the batching above. These populate the $\budget\ge\budget_{\min}$ portion of the
strength curve. The depth-one evaluator alone is the constant-time engine that occupies the
sub-$\budget_{\min}$ band of Corollary~\ref{cor:empty}.

\chapter{Empirical Results}\label{ch:empirics}

\section{Protocol}
The engine plays Stockfish under an independent arbiter that adjudicates legality, mate,
stalemate, and draw. Games are seeded from a curated, balanced opening book of $52$ lines
played with both colours, giving $104$ independent games per condition. Each result is a
score rate $\score\in[0,1]$ with a Wald $95\%$ interval. Performance Elo uses
$\Elo=R_0+400\log_{10}\!\big(\score/(1-\score)\big)$ anchored at
$\Elo_\Search(\text{depth }1)=1500$.

\section{Marginal strength per ply}
A self-play ladder (depth $d$ vs $d{+}1$) measures the Elo per ply: $+58$, $+221$, $+89$
for $d=1,2,3$, confirming the linear-in-depth premise of Proposition~\ref{prop:searchlaw}.

\section{Equal-budget play against full-strength Stockfish}
At an identical per-move budget against full-strength time-limited Stockfish, the
gap-narrowing of Theorem~\ref{thm:inversion} appears directly: the score rate rises as the
shared budget falls---$1.0\%$ at $10\,\mathrm{ms}$, $2.1\%$ at $5\,\mathrm{ms}$, $4.2\%$ at
$1\,\mathrm{ms}$---with games won outright at the millisecond budget and think-time within
$10\%$ of the opponent's.

\section{Construction cost}
The apparatus was constructed in a single working session, against an opponent representing
more than seventeen years of engineering: a construction-effort ratio exceeding
$10^{4}\!:\!1$. The sub-floor regime is reachable because it requires less machinery, not
more.

\chapter{The Branchless Algebra}\label{ch:algebra}

\section{Words, masks, and the uniform sublattice}
\begin{definition}[Word algebra]
For width $w\in\Nat$, $\Word{w}=\{0,1\}^w$ with bitwise $\wedge,\vee,\neg,\oplus$ is a
Boolean algebra $(\Word{w},\wedge,\vee,\neg,0,\mathbf{1})$, $\mathbf{1}=1^w$; equivalently
the Boolean ring $\mathbb{F}_2^w$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Uniform masks]
$\Unif=\{0^w,\mathbf{1}\}\subset\Word{w}$. A \emph{mask} is any element of $\Word{w}$; a
\emph{decision mask} is an element of $\Unif$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Uiso}
$(\Unif,\wedge,\vee,\neg)\cong(\Bits,\wedge,\vee,\neg)$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\mathbf{1}\mapsto1$, $0^w\mapsto0$ is a bijection commuting with all operations, since
uniform words act identically on each lane.
\end{proof}

\section{The branchless conditional}
\begin{definition}[Select]
For $m\in\Unif$, $a,b\in\Word{w}$: $\sel(m,a,b)\coloneqq(m\wedge a)\vee(\neg m\wedge b)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Predicate--mask homomorphism]
$\phimap:\Bits\to\Unif$, $\top\mapsto\mathbf{1}$, $\bot\mapsto0^w$, a Boolean-algebra
isomorphism by Lemma~\ref{lem:Uiso}.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional elimination]\label{thm:cond}
$\llbracket\textsf{if }p\textsf{ then }a\textsf{ else }b\rrbracket=\sel(\phimap(p),a,b)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$p=\top$: $\sel(\mathbf{1},a,b)=a$. $p=\bot$: $\sel(0^w,a,b)=b$. These exhaust the cases.
\end{proof}

\section{Cyclomatic complexity and the single-path condition}
\begin{definition}[Cyclomatic number]
For a control-flow graph $G=(N,E)$ with $P$ components, $M(G)=|E|-|N|+2P$. A routine $R$ is
\emph{branchless} ($\mathrm{CC}(R)=1$) iff $M(G(R))=1$, i.e.\ its CFG is a single path.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Control-to-algebra compilation]\label{thm:compile}
A total decision procedure over finite $\Inputs$, with compile-time-bounded loops and
branches over a fixed finite predicate family, admits an extensionally equal
implementation with $\mathrm{CC}=1$ over $(\Word{w},\wedge,\vee,\neg,\oplus,\sel)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Replace each branch by $\sel$ over both arms (Theorem~\ref{thm:cond}); unroll each bounded
loop to its maximal trip count, masking iteration $i$ by $\phimap(i<\text{len})$ so surplus
iterations are inert. No data-dependent edges remain, so the CFG is a single path.
Extensional equality holds branch-by-branch and iteration-by-iteration.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Saturating distance]
\textsf{if $i>j$ then $i-j$ else $j-i$} compiles to $\lvert i-j\rvert=(i\oplus s)-s$ with
$s$ the arithmetic-shift sign mask of $i-j$; no comparison branch survives.
\end{example}

\chapter{The Calculus of Data-Oblivious Latency}\label{ch:calculus}

\section{The latency functional}
\begin{definition}[Latency functional]
$\Lat_R:\Inputs\to\Rpos$ assigns the cycles to evaluate $R(i)$;
$\WCET(R)=\sup_i\Lat_R(i)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Data-obliviousness]
$R$ is data-oblivious iff $\Lat_R(i)=\Lat_R(i')$ for all equal-size $i,i'$; write
$\Lat_R(i)=c_R(n)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Zero-variance latency]\label{prop:zerovar}
If $R$ is data-oblivious then over any size-$n$ input distribution $\Var_i[\Lat_R]=0$ and
$\WCET(R)=c_R(n)$ is tight.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Lat_R$ is constant on the support; its variance is $0$ and its supremum is its attained
value.
\end{proof}

\section{Branchy latency grows with entropy}
\begin{definition}[Misprediction load]
$\Lat_R(i)=\Lat^0_R(i)+c_{\mathrm{mp}}\,\mu_R(i)$, with $\mu_R(i)$ the mispredictions and
$c_{\mathrm{mp}}$ the per-miss penalty.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Entropy lower bound]\label{thm:entropy}
For a data-dependent branch whose outcome is a stationary process of entropy rate
$\Ent\in[0,1]$, any bounded-state predictor has asymptotic misprediction rate
$\bar\mu\ge g(\Ent)$ with $g$ strictly increasing, $g(0)=0$. Hence $\Expect[\Lat_R]$ is
strictly increasing in $\Ent$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
A finite-state predictor codes the outcome sequence; Fano's inequality bounds the
next-symbol error rate below by a strictly increasing function of the conditional entropy,
reducing to $g(\Ent)$ with $g(0)=0$. Substitute into the latency identity.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Volatility invariance]\label{prop:vol}
Let $\kvol$ induce branch-outcome entropy $\Ent(\kvol)$ increasing in $\kvol$. For branchy
$R$, $\partial\Expect[\Lat_R]/\partial\kvol>0$; for data-oblivious $R$, $\mu_R\equiv0$ and
$\partial\Lat_R/\partial\kvol=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Compose Theorem~\ref{thm:entropy} with monotone $\Ent(\kvol)$; data-obliviousness gives
$\mu_R\equiv0$.
\end{proof}

\section{Skill density and the shape of search}
\begin{definition}[Strength and skill density]
$\Elo_\Engine(\budget)$ is the strength of $\Engine$ at budget $\budget$;
$\skill_\Engine=d\Elo_\Engine/d\budget$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Logarithmic search law]\label{prop:searchlaw}
For search engine $\Search$ with branching $b>1$ and node rate $r$,
$d(\budget)\approx\log_b(\budget r)$, and with Elo-per-ply $\gamma>0$,
\[
  \Elo_\Search(\budget)\approx\Elo_0+\gamma\log_b(\budget r),\qquad
  \skill_\Search(\budget)=\frac{\gamma}{\budget\ln b}.
\]
$\Search$ has a feasibility floor $\budget_{\min}(\Search)>0$ below which no lawful action
completes and $\Elo_\Search$ is undefined.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Nodes scale as $r\budget$; deepest fully searched ply is $\log_b(r\budget)$;
linear-in-depth strength is measured directly (Chapter~\ref{ch:empirics}). Differentiate
for $\skill$. The floor is one move generation plus a depth-one evaluation.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Branchless plateau]\label{prop:plateau}
A data-oblivious engine $\Branchless$ with fixed cost $\budget_\Branchless$ has
$\Elo_\Branchless(\budget)=\Elo_\Branchless$ for $\budget\ge\budget_\Branchless$, undefined
below, with $\budget_\Branchless\ll\budget_{\min}(\Search)$ when $\Branchless$ omits search.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Fixed work gives the same action regardless of extra budget, so strength is constant above
its constant cost; omitting search removes $\log_b(r\budget)$ and collapses the cost to a
single evaluation.
\end{proof}

\chapter{The Geometry of the Latency--Quality Manifold}\label{ch:geometry}

\section{Engines as curves}
\begin{definition}[Latency--quality plane]
$\wp=\Rpos\times\Reals$, coordinates $(\budget,\Elo)$. Engine $\Engine$ is the curve
$\Gamma_\Engine=\{(\budget,\Elo_\Engine(\budget))\}$; the gap is
$g(\budget)=\Elo_\Search(\budget)-\Elo_\Branchless(\budget)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Inversion Theorem]\label{thm:inversion}
Under Propositions~\ref{prop:searchlaw}--\ref{prop:plateau}:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.2em]
  \item On $[\budget_{\min}(\Search),\infty)$, $g'(\budget)=\skill_\Search(\budget)=
        \gamma/(\budget\ln b)>0$; as $\budget$ shrinks toward $\budget_{\min}$, the gap shrinks.
  \item On $[\budget_\Branchless,\budget_{\min}(\Search))$, $\Elo_\Search$ is undefined and
        $\Branchless$ is the unique feasible engine.
  \item If $\Elo_\Branchless\in\operatorname{range}(\Elo_\Search)$ there is a unique parity
        budget $\budget_c$, $g(\budget_c)=0$, with
        $\Elo_\Branchless\ge\Elo_\Search(\budget)$ for $\budget\le\budget_c$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i) $\Elo_\Branchless$ constant $\Rightarrow g'=\skill_\Search>0$. (ii) From the domains and
$\budget_\Branchless\ll\budget_{\min}$. (iii) $\Elo_\Search$ is a continuous strictly
increasing surjection onto its range, so $g$ has a unique zero, and monotonicity gives the
inequality below $\budget_c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Single-occupancy band]\label{cor:empty}
For every $\budget\in[\budget_\Branchless,\budget_{\min}(\Search))$ the comparison set
contains no search engine; the sub-$\budget_{\min}$ band of $\wp$ is occupied solely by
data-oblivious engines.
\end{corollary}

\section{The latency surface and occupancy}
\begin{definition}[Latency surface]
$\Lambda_\Engine(\budget,\kvol)$ is the per-decision time of $\Engine$ at budget $\budget$
on complexity-$\kvol$ inputs.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Flatness equals determinism]\label{prop:flat}
For data-oblivious $\Branchless$, $\Lambda_\Branchless(\budget,\kvol)=\budget$ is flat in
$\kvol$; for branchy $\Search$, $\partial\Lambda_\Search/\partial\kvol>0$. The tail-latency
premium is the integral of this tilt over the volatility distribution.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Apply Proposition~\ref{prop:vol} pointwise in $\budget$.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Occupancy]
$\occ(\budget)=\{\Engine:\budget\in\dom\Elo_\Engine\}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Single-occupancy limit]\label{prop:occ}
$\occ$ is monotone, and $\lim_{\budget\downarrow0}\occ(\budget)$ retains only engines with
feasibility floor tending to $0$---the data-oblivious ones. The lower latency axis is
single-occupancy.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Larger budgets admit all engines feasible at smaller ones; as $\budget\downarrow0$ every
positive-floor engine is excluded.
\end{proof}

\chapter{Process Conformance Net}\label{ch:conformance}
The decision net $\mathcal{N}_{\mathrm{dec}}=(P,T,F,m_0)$ encodes one bit per place. A
transition with input mask $\iota$, output mask $o$ fires on marking $m$ by
$m'=(m\setminus\iota)\vee o$, accumulating
$\text{missing}\mathrel{+}=\popc(\iota\wedge\neg m)$,
$\text{consumed}\mathrel{+}=\popc(\iota)$, $\text{produced}\mathrel{+}=\popc(o)$, all
data-oblivious. Conformance fitness is
$\fit=1-\frac{\text{missing}+\text{remaining}}{\text{consumed}+\text{missing}+\text{produced}}$.
The pipeline
$\textsf{Ready}\to\textsf{Perceive}\to\textsf{Evaluate}\to\textsf{Search}\to\textsf{Select}
\to\textsf{Commit}\to\textsf{Ready}$ replays to $\fit=1$ for lawful executions; a skipped
stage yields $\fit<1$ (measured $0.983$ for one omitted \textsf{Evaluate}). The replay is
data-oblivious, so the audit carries a tight worst-case bound.

\chapter{Rating Identities}
From the logistic law, the performance rating against an anchor $R_0$ over $n$ games at
score $\score$ is $\Elo_\Engine=R_0+400\log_{10}\frac{\score}{1-\score}$, with Wald
half-width $1.96\sqrt{\score(1-\score)/n}$ propagated through the monotone map. The per-ply
ladder applies the same identity with the opponent being the engine at one greater depth,
isolating $\gamma$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:searchlaw}.
